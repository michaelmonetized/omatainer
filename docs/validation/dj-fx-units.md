# Independently assigned DJ FX units

Open File → Project → DJ FX. Units A and B each have three independently enabled Echo, Reverb or Filter slots, visible wet amount and feedback/cutoff. Assign a unit to Deck A, Deck B, a named sampler destination or Master. Every assignment has its own stereo history. Assigning both a deck and Master processes that path twice; a sampler unit affects only the selected pad destination, retaining every other bus unchanged. Stable project/track references prevent deleted or replaced destinations from being reinterpreted.

Beat timing follows the current musical clock and the existing controller beat divisions. Manual timing sets exact milliseconds. The applied echo duration is visible, including the two-second capacity cap. The existing mapped FX encoder selects beat timing explicitly. Reverb decay and filter cutoff keep their existing normalized parameter controls. Both legacy controller banks and their stereo type/wet/parameter histories feed the same native unit state; no manufacturer address was added.

Deck Pre/Post placement is around the crossfader, with PFL retained before it. Sampler placement is around sampler volume and then follows its ordinary track route; Master placement is around master volume, before safety and limiting. Switch a slot or source off to fade new wet input over 2 ms and retain original tails. Paused decks continue draining their FX histories. A sampler unassignment drains to its original bus. Placement or sampler-destination changes require all slots off and settled tails. Choosing another type explicitly resets that slot. Closing the window leaves live controls active. Controls are transient, as were the existing surface banks; this checkpoint does not introduce stored DJ FX presets.

Fixed histories are prepared outside the callback and rebuilt at the actual output rate while retaining controls/routes and retiring old-rate input/tail ownership. The unique audio owner clears their generations when its stream stops. Inactive dry units bypass timing and processor work. Inactive histories retire after two seconds of samples below 1e-7; every echo interval fits within that quiet window.
